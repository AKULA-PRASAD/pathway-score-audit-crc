What do cancer pathway scores really measure in a bulk tumour?

Pathway scores such as "EMT" or "p53 activity" are read as tumour biology, but a bulk sample mixes cancer cells with stroma and immune cells, molecular subtypes and processing batches. This project shows when such scores mislead, provides a tool that checks them, and tests whether they predict recurrence in 452 stage II–III colon cancers.

01QuestionWhy it matters

Many expression signatures look predictive. Few survive honest validation.

After surgery for stage II–III colon cancer, a substantial minority of patients recur, and stage alone sorts that risk imperfectly. Gene-expression signatures are often reported to do better, but part of any apparent gain is optimism: a larger model always fits its own patients better than new ones. Fixed before any outcome was examined: do four biologically selected tumour-expression pathway scores add reproducible recurrence-discrimination information beyond established clinical variables in GSE39582?

03MethodFrom probes
to scores

A constrained, leakage-controlled design, fixed before outcomes

With 136 recurrences, selecting genes from the whole transcriptome would overfit: a null simulation gave an apparent C of 0.92 when the true value was 0.50. So the molecular question was limited to four pre-specified scores. Select a step to follow the data.

Models and validation

M1, clinical: age (spline), sex, stage II/III, tumour location and adjuvant chemotherapy, unpenalised.

M2, primary: M1 plus the four scores, with a ridge penalty on the molecular block only. A five-set model adding PI3K/AKT/mTOR was fixed in advance as a sensitivity analysis and never promoted.

Validation: an optimism-corrected nested bootstrap, five runs of 750 outer × 100 inner resamples, with the two models paired on the same resamples. The runs differ only in random seed: Monte Carlo replicates of one analysis, not independent experiments.

Technical: ridge λ is tuned by five-fold event-stratified cross-validation inside each training set (λ = 0.351 on the full data). Leaving the clinical block unpenalised keeps M1 exactly nested in M2 and the comparison scale-equivariant; shrinking the molecular block makes ΔC conservative. Each random draw has its own SeedSequence stream; checkpoints are append-only and hash-linked; reporting was gated on a frozen verifier.

05Bulk tissueWhat an array
actually measures

One array, many cell types: a bulk score is a mixture

Every expression array in this study was made from a piece of tumour tissue, not from isolated cancer cells. The mRNA of every cell in that piece is pooled into one measurement per gene, and a pathway score averages those measurements. Select a population, in the buttons or the illustration, to see how it could enter the score and what was and was not measured.

The EMT example: two biological stories, one number

Possibility 1Tumour cells in transition

Some tumour epithelial cells lose E-cadherin junctions and switch on mesenchymal genes such as vimentin and fibronectin.

→ ←
Bulk EMT scorethe same single number in both cases
conceptual

Possibility 2Stromal cells

Cancer-associated fibroblasts already express many of the same mesenchymal genes, so more stroma raises the score without any tumour-cell change.

An elevated bulk EMT-associated score does not establish tumour-cell EMT.

In this cohort the EMT hazard ratio was 1.070 (0.951–1.202): above 1 in every fit, never distinguishable from 1. Published work attributes much bulk mesenchymal signal to stroma (Calon 2015; Isella 2015). Cell composition was not measured in this project, but registered follow-up analyses estimated it computationally (ESTIMATE, MCP-counter): the EMT score correlates 0.95 with the fibroblast estimate, 0.94 with shared genes removed, within every molecular subtype, and the same holds in three independent cohorts (section 07). The evidence therefore favours Possibility 2 for the bulk signal, without excluding a tumour-cell component.

Its built-in survival screen gives a corrected gain of +0.0048 for the four scores on GSE39582, close to the frozen nested-bootstrap estimate of +0.0043. 08Subtype & batchTwo sources of
score variation

Scores track molecular subtype, and coefficients move with processing batch

Technical: batch and RNA-extraction location effects on the scores did not survive a permutation test stratified by the ComBat covariate combat_mod (0 of 10). That covariate matches recorded MMR status in all 410 samples, but its provenance is unresolved, so it is not an MMR variable. Four QC-flag dispersion effects survived; residual batch-associated structure remains on PC3 (ε² 0.061). A batch-stratified refit gave molecular HRs of 1.073, 0.912, 1.004 and 1.022 (point estimates).

Batch effects were characterised, not resolved.
